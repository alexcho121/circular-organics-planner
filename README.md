Circular Organics Planner (NSW)
Plan how a multi-tenant food building separates its food waste today, and how that setup should change by 2035.
Built for Climate Hack-tion 2026, "Build for 2035" challenge (Zero Waste & Methane Reduction). Guidance only — confirm with NSW EPA or your council.

Why it matters
Our COP31 priority area is Zero Waste & Methane Reduction.
Food in landfill produces methane. Each tonne kept out of landfill avoids roughly 30 to 75 kg of landfill methane.
Australia wastes around 7.6 million tonnes of food a year.
Australia's national target is to halve organic waste sent to landfill by 2030.
The Global Methane Pledge aims to cut methane by at least 30% from 2020 levels by 2030.
COP31 takes place in Antalya, Türkiye, in November 2026, with Australia as President of Negotiations.

What it is
Since 1 July 2026, NSW has required food businesses with a lot of general waste to separate their food waste.
A building manager has to answer five questions. The planner walks through them on one page:
Are we likely covered, and from when? 
A mandate check based on your general waste bins.
Which setup fits our building? 
A side-by-side comparison of three pathways.
Why this one? 
A rule-based recommendation with its reasons and what would change it.
What exactly do we do? 
Bins, collection days, and how many kilograms go where.
How should this change by 2035? 
A roadmap that shows when the best setup changes as waste grows.

Pathway
What it means
Off-site FOGO
Separate food waste and have it collected for off-site composting.
Hybrid
Process part of it on site, and send the rest off site.
On-site
Process most of it on site. Needs more space, a larger budget, and regulatory and safety checks.

Our key message: most of the methane saving comes from keeping food out of landfill. The pathway choice is about making that work in your building.

Who it's for
Managers of NSW buildings where several food tenants share a waste service, such as shopping centre food courts and university food precincts.
Where the building manager holds the waste contract, NSW EPA guidance expects them to work with tenants to manage food waste under the mandate. This tool is built for that person.
It is a planning aid. It is not a legal compliance decision.

How to use
Building profile. Enter your food tenants (or a total kg/week), general waste bins, waste room area, on-site space, local use, and budget. Or press Load small example / Load large example.
Mandate check. See whether you are likely covered now, from 2028, or from 2030.
Waste profile. Check the weekly food waste by tenant and in total.
Compare. See the three pathways side by side: bins, collections, cost, space, effort, and climate impact.
Recommendation. Read why this pathway was chosen, and what would change the answer.
Plan. Get bins, collection days, and off-site and on-site kg/week.
2035 roadmap. See how the recommendation changes as your food waste grows.
Assumptions & sources. See every value the tool used, with its range, source and confidence.
Tip: if you have weighed your food waste, enter the measured kg/week. It replaces the tool's estimate, which is much less reliable.

How it works
Mandate check. Weekly capacity = bin size × number of bins × collections per week, for general waste bins only. The thresholds follow the NSW EPA bin configurations (S3). The result always says "likely", because exemptions are not modelled.

Assumptions & limitations
We make our evidence and assumptions explicit.
39 settings, 23 references. Every value the app uses is in assumptions.csv, with a min, base and max, a source where one exists, and a confidence level: H (directly supported), M (modelled or vendor data), L (team assumption). Not every setting has its own source.
Food waste estimates are assumptions. The kg/week defaults per tenant type are team proposals, not NSW averages. A seven-day weighed audit is the best replacement.
The mandate check says "likely". It uses the NSW EPA bin capacity rules, but it does not model exemptions. Some exemptions exist, for example for eligible dining areas until 2030 (kitchens stay covered), for eligible on-site pre-processing, and for some regional areas. Check your site against the current NSW EPA list.
Two separate climate numbers. Landfill methane avoided (kg CH4/year) and net GHG saving (t CO2e/year). Both are model estimates based on scenarios from a 2023 NSW EPA technology assessment. Ranges are planning scenarios, not statistical confidence intervals. Net saving can be negative. Avoided methane builds up over time, not all in the year of diversion.
The Hybrid and On-site factors assume dehydration with the residue composted off site. Other technologies would give different results.
On-site processing needs site checks. Dehydrated food output is not automatically compost. Check approvals, ventilation, fire safety and trade waste rules for the exact device. A local use does not by itself make a pathway lawful.
Costs are indicative. Collection costs use one Sydney supplier's public prices (ex GST). Equipment budgets are ranges, not quotes. No rebate is assumed.
Space bands are rough. They do not include clearances, ventilation or storage.
The roadmap is simple. It grows food waste and bin capacity by your annual rate. Other inputs stay the same.
Impact and next steps
Managers get an explainable plan in minutes, before they ask any vendor for a quote.
How it could be tested: pilot one food court or campus, run a four-week weighed audit, apply the plan, then measure kilograms diverted, bin lifts and contamination against the planner's ranges. Feed the results back into the assumptions sheet.
Route to use: one pilot site, then facility-management companies that run many centres, then councils and NSW business programs, as a planning aid alongside EPA guidance.
